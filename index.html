Audio Transcribe

Turn a video into audio, then audio into a written Markdown transcript with speaker labels.

Your files stay on your computer. Everything runs inside this browser tab. Your video and audio are never uploaded. (The page does download the program and the speech models it needs, which are not your files.) Use Chrome or Edge on a computer.

1Video to audio

What this does: A video file holds pictures and sound together. Step 1 keeps only the sound and saves it as a WAV file, which is plain, uncompressed audio that speech software can read. The tool that does it is called ffmpeg. Your video is not changed; this makes a new file.

2Audio to a Markdown transcript

What this does: A speech-recognition model (Whisper) listens and types out the words with time stamps. A second pair of models listens for changes in voice and labels who is speaking (Speaker 1, Speaker 2). The result is a Markdown file: plain text that any AI tool or word processor can open. The labels are guesses; always check them against the recording.

    

  

  
Transcripts are machine drafts. Verify against the recording before relying on them. Browser limits: video files up to about 1.5 GB and recordings up to 40 minutes.
